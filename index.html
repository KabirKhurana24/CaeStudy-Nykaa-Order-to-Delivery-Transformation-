Executive view

The challenge is in the handoffs.

Nykaa has already built scale, regional reach and strong digital demand. The opportunity is to make the operational handoffs between inventory, warehouses, carriers and returns work as one system.

01 / COST

Fulfillment is growing faster than revenue

FY26 fulfillment expense increased 29.2% year-on-year to Rs 959 crore, compared with 26.1% revenue growth. Q2 FY27 intensity reached 10.11% of net revenue.

02 / COMPLEXITY

One customer order crosses many nodes

Regional warehouses, Nykaa Now dark stores, physical stores, marketplace sellers and external 3PL partners operate at different speeds and with different constraints.

03 / EXPERIENCE

Exceptions take too long to resolve

COD failures, late NDR action, split parcels and manual returns make orders more expensive while adding uncertainty for customers.

Working principle: keep the existing AWS and SAP backbone. Add a DOM and unified inventory orchestration layer around it, improve warehouse execution, and automate the exception paths before attempting network-wide change.

B1 / INVENTORY

Split-shipment proliferation

Fragmented inventory views across warehouses, stores and dark stores lead to multi-node allocation, duplicate freight and staggered arrivals.

B2 / COD & ADDRESS

Low-intent orders reach the pick queue

Static COD rules and weak address validation can send risky orders into warehouse operations before intent is confirmed.

B3 / WAREHOUSE

Manual pick, expiry and packing checks

Similar shades, batch expiry and fragile products need reliable scan verification and objective packing evidence.

B4 / DELIVERY

Exceptions are acted on too late

Failed delivery attempts and mid-transit cancellation gaps turn recoverable orders into costly RTOs.

B5 / RETURNS

Refund approval waits on several steps

Different policy windows, manual photo review, reverse pickup and warehouse QC stretch the refund cycle to 7–12+ days.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Some root causes still need validation

Inventory sync latency, precise COD RTO rates, pick-error levels and Nykaa Now unit economics are not public. They are internal Phase 1 data requests, not audited facts.

One orchestration layer across the network.

The proposal adds coordination and visibility without replacing the core SAP financial ledger or the AWS storefront. Existing platforms remain the backbone; new capabilities connect the operating seams.

PILLAR 1

Unified inventory and DOM

Use availability, zone distance, SLA, carrier rate cards and hub backlog to select the lowest landed-cost fulfillment node.

PILLAR 2

COD and NDR risk controls

Score COD orders before release, validate addresses, then act on failed delivery events while the order can still be saved.

PILLAR 3

Scan-based warehouse quality

Make bin, item and batch checks part of the pick workflow, with video evidence for fragile packing and disputes.

PILLAR 4

Returns and refund redesign

Offer one guided flow across verticals, while keeping risk-based physical QC holds for high-value or suspicious returns.

ENABLER

Integration, not rip-and-replace

Connect SAP, AWS storefronts, POS, ClickPost, 3PL APIs, payment gateways and Zendesk with governed interfaces.

ENABLER

People and adoption

Use vernacular training, floor coaches, store P&L credits and agreed exception ownership to make the changes stick.

05 / Delivery sequence

Two tracks. One 90-day proof point.

Do not cut over the whole network at once. Deliver cloud quick wins nationally while using a tightly controlled three-hub metro pilot to prove the physical operating changes.

DAYS 1–30 / PHASE 1

Discover and validate

  • Extract ERP, OMS, ClickPost and Zendesk telemetry.
  • Baseline split shipments, COD RTO, pick errors, labor hours and refund TAT.
  • Run time-motion studies in three pilot warehouses.
  • Finalize BRD, NFRs, DOM rules, store P&L credit and change plan.
  • Procure pilot RF scanners and video QC kits; clean SKU / EAN master data.

Gate M1: verified baseline, BRD and pilot budget. Close data gaps.

DAYS 31–60 / PHASE 2

Build and train

  • Build COD risk, geocoding, WhatsApp confirmation and ClickPost NDR / cancel APIs.
  • Configure inventory event bus and DOM split suppression.
  • Fit out three pilot hubs with RF/FEFO, Wi-Fi and video QC.
  • Build returns portal, Zendesk widget and Control Tower v1.
  • Run SIT and peak-load tests at 3,500 orders per minute; train pilot teams.

Gate M2: 100% SIT, load test and staff readiness.

DAYS 61–90 / PHASE 3

Launch and prove

  • Complete formal 10-scenario UAT and close defects.
  • Ramp Track A pan-India at 10%, 50% and 100% from Days 71–75.
  • Run Track B at 3 hubs, 6 stores and 5 dark stores from Days 75–85.
  • Use 24×7 floor hypercare and parallel Control Tower monitoring.
  • Complete Day 85–90 pilot value audit and national rollout decision.

Gate M3/M4: zero Sev-1 outages, ≥99.5% pilot pick accuracy and verified directional gains.

TRACK A / CLOUD QUICK WINS

Pan-India by Day 75

COD scoring, address geocoding, WhatsApp buyer confirmation, ClickPost NDR salvage and mid-transit cancellation APIs. No warehouse hardware or floor retraining required.

TRACK B / PHYSICAL PILOT

Three metro warehouses by Day 90

DOM, inventory event bus, RF/FEFO scanning, pack video QC and ship-from-store. Proposed pilot geography: Mumbai/Bhiwandi, Delhi NCR and Bengaluru, with adjacent stores and Nykaa Now dark stores.

Seasonal protection: avoid Pink Summer Sale cutover windows. Use staged A/B ramps for cloud features and parallel monitoring for warehouse changes.

07 / Governance & risk

Protect the rollout from its own side effects.

Operational improvements can introduce new problems if teams optimize only one metric. These mitigations are part of the design, not an afterthought.

Over-restricting COD in Tier 2 / Tier 3 pincodes

Use A/B holdout cohorts and monitor genuine buyer conversion, not only RTO reduction.

Peak-season cutover disruption

Respect sale freeze windows, stage release ramps and keep three-hub pilot gates before national physical rollout.

Picker productivity dip during RF rollout

Provide vernacular training under 30 minutes, floor coaches and temporary surge buffers.

Store resistance to ship-from-store

Agree store P&L attribution and maintain the two-unit safety floor before exposing inventory to online orders.

High-value return fraud or hygiene risk

Use risk-tiered instant refund rules; keep warehouse QC holds for high-value items and require doorstep photo plus barcode.

Bad data and integration gaps

Validate SKU/EAN masters, POS links and 3PL webhook reliability; buffer events when connectivity drops.

Decision rule: national rollout capex follows verified directional reduction in split shipments, COD RTO and pick errors, with zero Severity-1 outages in the pilot.
08 / Boundaries & evidence

What this case does - and does not - claim.

The capstone is an external diagnostic based on public information through Q2 FY27, not an internal audit of Nykaa's warehouse systems or actual unit economics.

Included in Project Velocity-O2D

DOM and unified inventory across Beauty, Fashion and Nykaa Now; COD risk and address validation; RF / FEFO warehouse workflows; ClickPost and 3PL NDR / cancellation APIs; returns and exchange; O2D Control Tower and Zendesk integration.

Explicitly outside scope

Replacing the SAP financial ERP ledger or consumer storefront; greenfield warehouses or heavy fixed robotics; GCC / Nysaa fulfillment and Aramex customs workflows; Superstore B2B field-force CRM; brand procurement margin negotiations.

Data gaps to close in Phase 1: exact WMS vendors, bin-level accuracy, picker productivity, pincode-level COD RTO, internal refund TAT, Zendesk handling cost and Nykaa Now unit economics. These values should be replaced with verified internal telemetry before financial sign-off.
